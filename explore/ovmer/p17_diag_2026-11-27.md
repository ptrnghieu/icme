# P17 OV-MERD+: label-free diagnostics (2026-11-27)

No ground truth was read. Inputs: P17 labels (extractor seed 0, seed 1, old prompt) for 532 clips × 16 samples.
Scripts: `p17_diag_labelfree.py` (parts 1–2; part 3 in it has a wheel-index bug, fixed in `p17_diag_search_gap.py`).
Both are run from the scratchpad, where `p17r/` holds the outputs (not committed: MER-derived).

| # | Question | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How much of the between-sample label disagreement comes from the extractor? | Utility between two extractions (seed 0 vs seed 1) of the **same** description: 0.903 (identical label set in 77.1%). Utility between two **different** descriptions of the same clip: 0.527. Extractor share of the disagreement: **20.6%**; descriptions (MLLM sampling): 79.4%. |
| 2 | Does the "at most four labels" sentence make the samples sparser? | Labels per sample 2.69 (rl prompt) vs 2.76 (old prompt); in-vocabulary 2.23 vs 2.22; level-1 groups per sample (wheel 1) **1.38 vs 1.38**. Only the >4 tail shrinks (8.6% → 3.3%). Group density is unchanged. |
| 3 | Is greedy search the bottleneck? | Over the same 12-label pool with \|Y\| ≤ 6, greedy reaches the exhaustive argmax of R-hat in **97.0%** of clips; mean R-hat gap 0.0002 (99th percentile 0.006). Consistency check: the bitmask R-hat equals `sample_f` exactly on the greedy sets. |
| 4 | Does the 12-candidate pool cap matter for the objective? | Unlimited pool changes the selection in 10.2% of clips; mean R-hat gain **0.0008**. |

Conclusion: the search and the candidate pool have almost no headroom on the current objective. The gap between 68.91 and the oracle 91.48 lies in the objective (the references and the utility) and in the model's distribution.
